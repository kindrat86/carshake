Methodology — In One Paragraph

The Valet Damage Index ranks 40 US cities by an estimated composite valet-related vehicle-damage incident rate (incidents per 1,000 valet visits). The estimate synthesizes publicly available signals: city parking-authority valet-permit density, state insurance-department claims filings, NHTSA parking-related incident statistics, valet-service density, and community-sourced driver reports. It is a directional ranking, not a direct measurement — actual rates vary by venue, time of day, vehicle type, and operator. Average claim values are estimated from insurance-industry data.

For fact-checkers: we describe these numbers as "estimated" and "directional" throughout. We do not claim primary survey data. The full methodology and per-field definitions are on the original dataset page.

Story Angles

  • Local angle: "How does [your city] rank for valet damage?" — every city has its own data card, e.g. Austin, Miami, Chicago.
  • The documentation gap: fewer than 15% of valet-damage claimants arrive with timestamped proof — and those who do succeed ~3.4× more often (per the claims benchmark).
  • Geographic inequality: the highest-rate city (New York) sees roughly 10× the valet-damage rate of the lowest (Sacramento).
  • Enforcement gap: only 3 of 40 cities have "strict" valet enforcement; 21 are classified "loose."
