Broken Side Mirror Repair Cost: The Full Picture

A broken side mirror runs $40 for a stick-on replacement glass to $1,500+ for a power-folding, heated, blind-spot-camera assembly. Like tail lights, the cost is all about the tech packed into the part.

Replacement mirror glass only

$40–$120

If the housing is intact and only the glass is cracked, a stick-on or clip-in replacement glass is cheap and DIY-able in 10 minutes.

Manual mirror assembly

$80–$250

A basic manual-adjust mirror housing and glass. Bolt-on replacement, no wiring complexity.

Power mirror assembly

$150–$500

Power-adjustable mirror with heating. Part cost rises with motor and heating elements; labor includes door-panel removal and wiring.

Power-folding / blind-spot mirror

$400–$1,200

Folding motors, turn-signal repeaters, and blind-spot warning lights add electronics. Luxury vehicles run to the top of the range.

Mirror w/ side camera (ADAS)

$800–$1,500+

Mirrors with integrated cameras (e.g., for 360° view or lane-change assist) require recalibration after replacement, adding $150–$400.

Does insurance cover a broken side mirror?

It depends on the cause. A mirror clipped in a tight parking garage or by a passing car is a collision claim subject to your deductible — often not worth filing for a $200 manual mirror. If it happened in a valet's custody, their liability is the better target, provided you can prove the mirror was intact at handover.

How do I prove a valet broke my side mirror?

Side mirrors are one of the most common valet damage items because of tight garage lanes. A pre-handover scan that captures both mirrors (folded and extended), then a post-pickup scan of the broken mirror, is the proof. Mirrors fold and unfold in transit, so photographing them in their parked position at handover establishes the baseline.
