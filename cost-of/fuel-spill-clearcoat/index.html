Fuel Spill Clear Coat Damage Repair Cost

Gasoline or solvent spilled on a fender eats through the clear coat in minutes, leaving a dull, stained patch. A light spill can be compounded and resealed for $150; a soak that's bubbled the paint means a $400–$1,500 panel respray.

Light spill, no etching (compound+seal)

$100–$250

If caught quickly, a detailer can machine-polish the haze out and reapply a ceramic or sealant topcoat. No paint work.

Moderate spill, dull patch

$200–$450

Clear coat partially etched. Wet-sanding, polishing, and resealing can restore clarity if the base coat is intact.

Severe spill, clear coat failure

$400–$900

The clear coat has bubbled or lifted. The panel needs to be sanded to bare, re-primed, re-based, and re-cleared.

Full panel respray (heavily soaked)

$700–$1,500+

When fuel ran down the quarter panel and pooled at a body line, the damage often spans the whole panel and blending into the adjacent panel is needed.

Is fuel-spill paint damage covered by insurance?

Generally no — it's usually classed as a chemical or environmental exposure, not a collision or comprehensive peril. If the spill happened at a full-service gas station or in a valet/shop's care, their general liability is the target, but you'll need to prove the paint was intact beforehand and that the spill happened on their watch.

How do I prove fuel-spill damage happened at handover?

A pre-handover scan of the rear quarter panels — the most common place fuel dribbles during fill-up — is the baseline. A timestamped photo of clean, glossy paint at drop-off, then a photo of the dull, stained patch at pickup, attributes the damage. Spill damage is one of the easiest to win because the visual change is dramatic.
