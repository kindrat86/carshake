How Much Does a Door Ding Cost to Fix?

A door ding costs anywhere from $100 to $500+ to fix. The number you actually pay depends on one thing: whether the paint is cracked. Paintless dent repair can erase a clean ding in under an hour; a cracked-paint ding becomes a body-shop job.

Small ding, paint intact (PDR)

$100–$250

Paintless dent repair uses specialized tools to massage the metal back into shape from behind the panel. No sanding, no painting, done in an hour or two.

Larger ding, paint intact (PDR)

$150–$350

Bigger dents (golf-ball size and up) take longer and may require glue-pulling, but still avoid the paint shop if the finish is unbroken.

Ding with cracked or chipped paint

$300–$600

Once the paint is broken, it becomes a body-shop job: filler, primer, base coat, clear coat, and blending into the surrounding panel.

Deep crease dent with paint damage

$500–$1,000

Sharp creases stretch the metal and often crack the paint. Expect filler, multiple paint stages, and a full day at the shop.

Will my insurance cover a door ding?

It depends on the cause. If another driver dinged your parked car and left, that is usually a collision claim subject to your deductible ($500–$1,000), and a $200 dent is cheaper to fix yourself than to file. If the ding happened in a valet's or shop's custody, their garagekeeper's or general liability policy should cover it, but only if you can prove it occurred on their watch.

How do I prove a valet caused the door ding?

With a timestamped before-and-after photo record of your car at handover. Without a pre-handover scan showing the door was clean, the valet will claim the ding was already there, and you have no recourse. A 60-second scan before you hand over the keys is the single highest-leverage thing you can do.
