Bumper Scrape Repair Cost: The Real Numbers

A bumper scrape runs from $150 for a buffable scuff to $2,000+ for a full repaint or replacement. The biggest cost driver is not the scrape itself — it is the parking sensors, cameras, and radar hidden behind modern bumpers.

Light clear-coat scuff (buff)

$100–$250

If the scrape only went through the clear coat, a detailer can wet-sand and buff it out in under an hour. No paint matching needed.

Paint transfer, no clear-coat break

$150–$350

Another car's paint rubbed off onto your bumper. Compounding and polishing removes the transfer; a light clear-coat touch-up seals it.

Deep scrape through the paint

$400–$800

Sanding, primer, base coat, clear coat, and blending. Most body shops charge a panel minimum of $300–$500 regardless of scrape size.

Full bumper repaint

$600–$1,200

When the scrape spans most of the bumper or the existing paint is aged, a full repaint (remove, strip, paint, reinstall) looks better than a patch.

Bumper cover replacement + sensors

$900–$2,000+

A torn cover needs replacement. Add sensor recalibration if the scrape cracked a parking-sensor or radar housing — calibration alone can run $150–$400.

Is a bumper scrape worth an insurance claim?

Usually not, unless the repair exceeds your deductible. A $400 scrape on a $500 deductible means you pay the whole bill and a claim may raise your premium. The exception is when the scrape happened in a valet's or shop's custody — then their liability policy should pay, and your evidence (not your deductible) is what wins it.

How do I prove the valet scraped my bumper?

A pre-handover photo of the bumper, timestamped and geotagged, then a post-handover photo of the fresh scrape. The valet will otherwise claim pre-existing damage. Scanning the front and rear bumpers — the two highest-risk zones — before you hand over the keys is exactly what catches this.
