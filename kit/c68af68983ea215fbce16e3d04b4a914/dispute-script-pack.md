# CarShake Vehicle Damage Dispute Script Pack

**Five practical templates for organizing a calm, evidence-led response**

Replace the bracketed fields. Include only facts you can support. These templates are educational, not legal advice, and cannot guarantee a result.

## Script 1: Valet Manager Email

**Subject:** Vehicle damage report - [DATE] - [VEHICLE / VALET TICKET]

Hello [MANAGER NAME],

I am reporting damage I noticed when my vehicle was returned by your valet on [DATE] at [LOCATION]. The affected area is [SHORT FACTUAL DESCRIPTION].

I have attached the relevant before-handover and after-pickup photos, the valet ticket, and the handover record. The files show [PRECISE OBSERVABLE DIFFERENCE].

Please confirm the incident reference, the person handling the review, and the next step in your claims process. Please also preserve any records relevant to this handover, including the ticket record and available operational records.

I would appreciate a written response by [REASONABLE DATE]. I am available at [PHONE / EMAIL] if you need a clearer copy of any attachment.

Thank you,
[NAME]

## Script 2: Hotel General Manager Escalation

**Subject:** Request for review of valet damage incident [REFERENCE]

Hello [GENERAL MANAGER NAME],

I am asking for your review of a vehicle damage incident connected to the valet service at [HOTEL] on [DATE]. I first reported it to [PERSON / DEPARTMENT] on [DATE], under reference [REFERENCE].

The unresolved issue is [ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY]. My evidence includes time-stamped before and after photos, the valet ticket or handover receipt, and the correspondence to date.

I am not asking you to assume responsibility without a review. I am asking for a clear written decision, the basis for that decision, and the applicable escalation or insurer process.

Please confirm who will review the evidence and when I should expect a response. The most relevant files are attached and labeled in chronological order.

Regards,
[NAME]

## Script 3: Rental Agency Dispute Email

**Subject:** Dispute of damage charge [AGREEMENT / CLAIM NUMBER]

Hello [CLAIMS TEAM / NAME],

I dispute the damage charge of [AMOUNT] connected to rental agreement [NUMBER] for [VEHICLE].

At pickup on [DATE / TIME], I documented [RELEVANT CONDITION]. At return on [DATE / TIME], I documented [RELEVANT CONDITION]. I reported [ANY PRE-EXISTING DAMAGE OR RETURN ISSUE] through [CHANNEL] and received [ACKNOWLEDGEMENT / REFERENCE].

Please provide the materials used to assess this charge, including:

1. The pickup and return inspection records
2. Dated photographs of the claimed damage
3. The repair estimate or invoice
4. The calculation and contractual basis of the amount charged
5. The written appeal or escalation process and deadline

I have attached the agreement, my pickup and return records, and the relevant acknowledgement. Please place the charge under review and reply in writing.

Regards,
[NAME]

## Script 4: Phone Call Guide

**Opening**

"Hello, I am calling about vehicle damage reference [REFERENCE] from [DATE]. I have the before and after records in front of me. I would like to confirm the review status and the next written step."

**If asked what happened**

"What I can verify is [SHORT FACTUAL DESCRIPTION]. My before record shows [FACT]. My after record shows [FACT]. I do not want to speculate beyond the evidence."

**Questions to ask**

- "What is the incident or claim reference?"
- "Who owns the review?"
- "Which evidence has been considered?"
- "Is anything missing from my submission?"
- "What is the expected response date?"
- "What is the written escalation process if we disagree?"

**Close**

"Thank you. Please send the reference and next steps to [EMAIL]. I will reply with a short written summary of this call so the record is clear."

**After the call**

Email a factual note with the date, time, number called, name or role of the representative, the agreed next step, and any deadline. Do not rely on memory alone.

## Script 5: Small-Claims Preparation List

Small-claims rules vary substantially by location. Check the official court or tribunal source for jurisdiction, limits, fees, service requirements, and deadlines before filing.

Organize:

- [ ] A one-page chronology with dates and events
- [ ] The agreement, ticket, receipt, and applicable terms
- [ ] Original before and after photo files
- [ ] A short index matching each image to the chronology
- [ ] Incident reports and claim references
- [ ] Emails, messages, and call notes
- [ ] Repair estimates, invoices, and proof of payment
- [ ] The other party's written decision or refusal
- [ ] Evidence of the amount requested and how it was calculated
- [ ] The correct legal name and address of the party you intend to name
- [ ] The official filing deadline and service method
- [ ] Copies prepared in the format required by the local process

Before filing, ask:

1. Can I prove the facts with original, organized evidence?
2. Have I followed the contract's and local process's required steps?
3. Is the amount supported rather than estimated loosely?
4. Have I identified the correct party and forum?
5. Is a negotiated resolution still practical?

## Evidence Attachment Order

Use a short, consistent package:

1. One-page chronology
2. Before images
3. Handover or pickup acknowledgement
4. After images
5. Incident report
6. Relevant correspondence
7. Estimate or invoice

Avoid sending an unfiltered photo dump. Each attachment should answer a specific factual question.
