Dent Repair Cost When the Paint Is Intact (PDR)

A dent where the paint is unbroken is the best-case scenario: paintless dent repair (PDR) can often restore the panel for $100–$350 without sanding, fillers, or repainting. Once the paint cracks, costs jump sharply.

Small dent, paint intact (PDR)

$100–$250

A golf-ball-sized dent on a flat panel. A PDR tech works the metal back from behind using specialized rods. Usually done in under an hour.

Large dent, paint intact (PDR)

$150–$350

Bigger dents take longer and may need glue-pulling, but PDR still avoids the paint shop as long as the finish is unbroken.

Dent on a body line / crease

$200–$400

Dents across sharp body creases are harder to massage out cleanly and take more skill (and time) to resolve via PDR.

Multiple dents (hail-style)

$75–$150 per dent

Volume pricing for many small dents (common with hail). See our hail-damage guide for storm-scale pricing.

Dent where PDR isn't possible

$400–$900

If the dent is on an edge, double-panel, or a plastic bumper, PDR may not be an option and it becomes a conventional body-shop repair.

Is a dent worth an insurance claim?

Usually not for a single dent under your deductible. A $200 PDR repair on a $500 deductible is an out-of-pocket job. The exception is a dent caused in a valet's or shop's custody — then their liability should cover it, and your proof (a pre-handover scan showing the clean panel) is what wins the claim.

How do I prove a valet caused the dent?

A pre-handover scan of every exterior panel, timestamped and geotagged, then a post-pickup scan showing the new dent. Without the before-record, the valet will claim pre-existing damage — which is often impossible to disprove after the fact. Sixty seconds of scanning before you hand over the keys is the entire game.
