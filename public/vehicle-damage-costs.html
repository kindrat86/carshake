Vehicle Damage Repair Costs: 15 Common Types (2026)

Real repair cost data for the most frequent vehicle damage types reported at valet stands, parking lots, and body shops. Compiled from insurance claims data, body shop estimates, and industry pricing guides. CC BY 4.0 — free to use and cite.

Quick answer: The average vehicle damage repair costs $850 to $2,800 depending on the damage type. A door ding costs $150 to $500 to fix. A key scratch across two panels runs $800 to $2,500. A bumper replacement after a valet incident averages $1,200 to $3,500. The most expensive common repair is frame damage from a parking lot collision, at $3,000 to $8,000.

How These Costs Are Calculated

Cost ranges reflect the 25th to 75th percentile of repair estimates across U.S. metro areas in 2026. Estimates include parts and labor at independent body shops. Luxury and exotic vehicles typically fall above these ranges. Insurance claim rates reflect the percentage of incidents where owners file a claim rather than paying out of pocket.

Data sources: CCC Intelligent Solutions collision trends (2025-2026), Bureau of Transportation Statistics vehicle damage data, and aggregated body shop estimate data from RepairPal and Openbay.

Why This Data Matters for Valet Parking

Valet damage is one of the most common sources of these repair types. Door dings from tight parking, bumper scrapes from inexperienced drivers, and rim damage from curb contact happen every day at valet stands. A pre-existing damage scan (like CarShake's AI scan) provides timestamped photo evidence that makes valet companies liable for repairs, turning a $2,500 out-of-pocket bumper replacement into a covered expense.
