Hail Damage Repair Cost: PDR vs Conventional

Hail damage ranges from $75 per dent for paintless dent repair (PDR) to $10,000+ for a severe storm that totals a car. PDR is the go-to because hail dents rarely break the paint — and it's almost always a comprehensive insurance claim.

Light hail (few dents, PDR)

$75–$150 per dent

A handful of small dents on flat panels. PDR techs work the dents out from behind using rods and glue-pulling. No paint, no fillers.

Moderate hail (full hood/roof)

$2,500–$5,000

Dozens of dents across the hood, roof, and trunk. PDR is still viable if the paint is intact; total labor time goes up sharply.

Severe hail (every panel)

$5,000–$10,000+

Large dents across every horizontal surface. Some panels may need conventional body work or replacement if the metal is stretched too thin for PDR.

Storm that cracks paint

$7,000–$15,000+

Once hail chips the paint, each dent becomes a body-shop repair. Severe cases often total older or lower-value vehicles.

Is hail damage worth an insurance claim?

Almost always yes — hail is a comprehensive peril and the repairs frequently exceed the deductible. Comprehensive claims for acts of nature typically do not raise premiums the way at-fault collision claims do. Document every dent with photos immediately after the storm, before any rental or lot can argue the dents pre-existed.

My car got hailed on while parked — who pays?

Your own comprehensive coverage is the reliable path; a lot or valet will usually not accept liability for weather. The risk is being blamed for pre-existing dents at your next rental return or trade-in — a timestamped scan after the hailstorm establishes that the dents existed on a specific date and weren't caused later by you.
