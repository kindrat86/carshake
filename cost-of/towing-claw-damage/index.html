Towing Damage Repair Cost: Hooks, Claws, and A-Frames

Towing damage — a gouged bumper from a claw, a torn undertray from a hook, or curb-rashed wheels from a careless A-frame — runs $200 for a light scuff to $3,000+ for torn bodywork or damaged suspension. Tow companies fight these claims hard, so documentation is everything.

Wheel-lift / A-frame scuffs

$100–$400

Wheel and bumper scuffs from the tow rig's contact points. Often refinishable via PDR or a light repaint.

Torn lower bumper / undertray

$400–$1,200

Hooks and claws routinely tear the lower valance, fog-light housings, and plastic undertrays. Replacement plus paint.

Damaged suspension / subframe

$1,000–$3,000+

An improperly rigged tow can bend a control arm or damage the subframe. Requires a full alignment and possibly suspension replacement.

Drivetrain damage (AWD/4WD)

$2,000–$5,000+

Towing an AWD or 4WD vehicle on its drive wheels destroys the transfer case or center diff. A flatbed was required — if they didn't use one, they're liable.

Who pays for towing damage?

The tow company is liable for damage caused by their own rigging or driving, but they fight claims aggressively and their receipts disclaim liability for pre-existing damage. Your own collision or comprehensive coverage can pay (subject to your deductible) and then subrogate against the tow company. The deciding factor is always proof of the car's condition before they hooked it.

How do I document my car's condition before a tow?

Before the truck leaves with your car, scan every panel, wheel, and the underbody area you can reach. A timestamped, geotagged record of the car's condition at the moment of pickup is the only thing that defeats a tow company's 'pre-existing damage' defense. Towing claims are among the most contested; the pre-tow scan is non-negotiable.
