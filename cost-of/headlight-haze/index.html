Headlight Restoration and Replacement Cost

Restoring a hazy, yellowed headlight costs $50–$150 professionally; replacing a modern LED or matrix headlight assembly runs $200 to $2,000+. Haze is a wear item you can fix; a cracked or failed unit is a part replacement.

DIY restoration kit

$15–$40

Sanding discs, polishing compound, and a UV clear coat. Works well for mild haze; results depend heavily on technique and on sealing the lens afterward.

Professional restoration

$50–$150 per pair

A detailer sands through the grits, polishes, and applies a durable UV-clear. Lasts 1–3 years depending on the topcoat quality.

Halogen assembly replacement

$100–$300

A conventional halogen headlight for an older or mainstream car. Part plus labor; no programming.

LED / HID assembly replacement

$300–$1,000

Modern LED or projector-HID units are sealed assemblies — you replace the whole unit, not a bulb. Part cost dominates.

Matrix / adaptive LED assembly

$1,000–$2,500+

Adaptive driving-beam and matrix LED units (luxury and newer EVs) each cost thousands and often require software recalibration after install.

Is headlight damage covered by insurance?

A cracked or smashed headlight from a collision or road debris is a comprehensive or collision claim, subject to your deductible. Pure haze and yellowing is wear and tear and is not covered — that is a maintenance cost. If a headlight was cracked during a handover, a pre-handover scan of the lights is what attributes it.

How do I prove headlight damage happened at handover?

Include the front lights in your pre-handover scan (they're easy to skip because they're at knee height). A timestamped photo of an intact headlight at drop-off, then a photo of the cracked lens at pickup, is the record that defeats a pre-existing-damage claim.
