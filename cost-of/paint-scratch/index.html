Car Paint Scratch Repair Cost: A Full Breakdown

A car scratch costs anywhere from $20 for a home touch-up pen to $1,500+ for a full panel respray. The price hinges on how deep the scratch is — clear coat, base coat, or primer — because each layer down means more labor and material.

Clear-coat scratch (buff)

$75–$200

A scratch that hasn't broken through to the color coat can often be wet-sanded and buffed out by a detailer. Fingernail test: if your nail barely catches, it's clear-coat depth.

Base-coat scratch (touch-up)

$100–$300

Through the clear coat into the color. A pro touch-up with matched paint and a clear-coat blend hides it well from a few feet away.

Deep scratch to primer/metal

$300–$800

Through the color into the primer or bare metal. Requires sanding, primer, base coat, clear coat, and blending into the adjacent panel so the new paint doesn't stand out.

Keyed scratch (long, deep)

$500–$1,500+

A key dragged across a door or quarter panel is usually deep and long, often crossing body lines. Full panel prep and respray, sometimes two panels to blend.

Multiple-panel scratch

$1,000–$2,500+

When a scratch runs across two or more panels (e.g., door + quarter), each panel is billed separately and the blending work multiplies.

Should I claim a paint scratch on insurance?

Only if the repair clearly exceeds your deductible and you can identify a responsible party. A $300 scratch on a $500 deductible is an out-of-pocket fix. Comprehensive coverage applies for vandalism (a keyed car), but you'll still pay the deductible and a claim can raise rates. For a scratch in a valet's or shop's custody, pursue their liability first with timestamped proof.

How do I prove a valet scratched my car?

Photograph every panel before handover with a timestamped, geotagged scan, then again at pickup. The before-scan is what defeats the standard “pre-existing damage” defense. Park scratches and valet scratches look identical at the end — only a pre-handover record separates the two.
