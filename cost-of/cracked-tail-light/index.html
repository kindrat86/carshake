Cracked Tail Light Repair Cost: What You'll Pay

A cracked tail light costs $50 for a DIY lens repair up to $600+ to replace a modern LED assembly. The cost swings wildly because a traditional bulb housing is cheap, while an integrated LED panel can cost hundreds just for the part.

Lens crack seal (DIY tape/kits)

$10–$30

Red lens tape or a UV-cure repair kit keeps water out and passes a roadside inspection temporarily. Not a permanent fix.

Aftermarket lens cover replacement

$50–$150

For older cars with separate, replaceable lens covers (not sealed LED units). Bolt-on, no programming.

OEM tail light assembly (bulb type)

$100–$350

A full sealed-assembly replacement for a conventional bulb housing. Part plus 30–60 minutes of labor.

LED / integrated tail light assembly

$300–$900

Modern LED panels are single sealed units — you replace the whole assembly, not a bulb. Luxury and EV tail lights run higher.

Tail light + body/paint repair

$400–$1,000+

A hard impact that cracked the light often also damaged the surrounding quarter panel. Add body work and paint to the assembly cost.

Will insurance pay for a cracked tail light?

Only if it's part of a larger covered incident (a collision or a hit while parked). A lone cracked tail light is usually under the deductible. If it happened in a valet's or lot's custody, their liability is the route — but you need to prove the light was intact at handover.

How do I prove a valet cracked my tail light?

Include all lights (front and rear) in your pre-handover scan. A timestamped photo of an intact tail light, then a photo of the cracked one at pickup, is the evidence that defeats a “pre-existing damage” claim. Tail lights are easy to overlook because drivers rarely photograph them — which is exactly why they're a common valet dispute.
